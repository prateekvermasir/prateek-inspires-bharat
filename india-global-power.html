India's Rise as a Global Power: The Nation Reshaping the 21st-Century World Order

How economic transformation, technological progress, defence development and independent diplomacy are strengthening India's position in an increasingly competitive world.

By Prateek Verma
Founder, Prateek Inspires Bharat
Category: Analysis

The global balance of power is changing. Economic competition, technological innovation, strategic rivalries and shifting international partnerships are transforming the way countries pursue their national interests. In this changing environment, India is becoming an increasingly important participant in discussions about the future of the international order.

With a large domestic market, a growing technology sector, expanding infrastructure and an influential diplomatic presence, India has several advantages that can support its long-term ambitions. Its challenge is to turn these advantages into sustained economic prosperity, stronger national capabilities and greater influence in international affairs.

India's long-term rise will depend not only on the size of its economy or military, but also on its ability to innovate, create opportunities and make independent strategic decisions.

1. Economic Power: The Foundation of Global Influence

Economic strength is one of the most important foundations of national power. A strong economy gives a country greater capacity to invest in infrastructure, education, research, public services and national security.

India has attracted international attention because of its economic growth, expanding consumer market and large services sector. Information technology, business services, manufacturing, pharmaceuticals and digital businesses contribute to the country's economic profile.

The World Bank reported that India's economy grew by 6.5 percent in the 2024–25 financial year. Maintaining strong and sustainable growth will require continued investment, higher productivity, reliable infrastructure and the creation of productive jobs.

India's next economic challenge is to ensure that growth creates broad-based opportunities. Better skills, competitive industries, stronger exports and greater participation in global supply chains can help transform economic expansion into long-term national strength.

2. Defence and Security: Building Greater Self-Reliance

National security is an essential part of strategic independence. A country that develops more of its own defence technology, equipment and industrial capacity can improve its ability to maintain readiness and respond to changing security requirements.

India has increasingly focused on domestic defence manufacturing, research and development, and collaboration between public institutions, private companies and technology innovators. These efforts are intended to strengthen the domestic industrial base and reduce excessive dependence on foreign suppliers.

According to India's Department of Defence Production, domestic defence production reached a reported record of ₹1.78 lakh crore in the 2025–26 financial year. Defence exports also reached ₹38,424 crore during that period, according to official figures.

These figures indicate the growing scale of India's defence industry. However, lasting self-reliance requires more than increased production. Research quality, advanced components, dependable supply chains, testing, maintenance and the ability to develop sophisticated technologies remain important priorities.

A stronger domestic defence ecosystem can support national security while creating opportunities for engineers, researchers, manufacturers and skilled workers.

3. Technology and Space: Investing in the Future

Technology increasingly influences economic competitiveness, public services and national security. Countries that develop advanced capabilities in digital infrastructure, artificial intelligence, semiconductors, clean energy and space applications may gain important long-term advantages.

India has developed a substantial digital services ecosystem and has expanded the use of digital platforms for payments, public services and business activity. Its technology sector also connects Indian professionals and companies with international markets.

India's space programme, led by the Indian Space Research Organisation (ISRO), has demonstrated the value of investing in scientific research and engineering. Satellite services, Earth observation, navigation and space exploration can contribute to agriculture, communications, disaster management and scientific knowledge.

The next phase will require sustained investment in research, stronger university-industry partnerships, support for startups and wider access to high-quality technical education. Technological progress must also be accompanied by attention to cybersecurity, privacy and responsible innovation.
